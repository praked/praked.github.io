SailSwarm is my PhD project at the University of Konstanz: a swarm of small
autonomous sailboats built for long-duration missions on open water. Wind is
free and a sailing robot can stay out far longer than a motorised one, but it
cannot go wherever it likes, whenever it likes. Getting one boat to sail itself
is hard; getting several to sail *together* is the question this project is
about.

## Latest from the lake

### Two boats, one lake

In September 2026 two of the robotic sailboats went out on Lake Constance
together for the first time. For this first multi-robot test both were still
running under motor power. The sails come next.

### Air, surface and underwater

A joint demo with Hun Jang, Liang Li and Heiko Hamann put three very different
robots into one shared environment: an autonomous sailboat on the surface, a
robotic fish below it and a quadrotor above. It is an early look at what a
swarm spanning several domains could do.

## Where this is going

- **Now:** two robots on the lake, under motor power.
- **Next:** motors off, sails up, sailing autonomously.
- **Then:** a swarm on the lake.

More will appear here as it happens.

## Earlier trials: a single boat

The first boat, Aqua Flash, learned the basics on its own: sailing manoeuvres,
holding a position against the wind, and sailing a course through several
points.
